# LD-0456 — the `X.1.0` second-exec-identity modality is deprecated

**Andrew ~17:09Z:** *"2.1.0_COO should not even exist that is a deprecated modality of exec and
should be deleted categorically"*

## 1. WHAT THE MODALITY IS, AND WHY IT IS A DEFECT RATHER THAN A STYLE

A `X.1.0_SA_*` serial is a **spare exec identity** minted to run a second concurrent instance of an
exec role. The correct mechanism already exists and is law — CLAUDE.md Rule 27 seating, his own
words: *"we can simply bring on `~tilde` agents if there are multiple projects we want to pursue at
once."* **A second COO is `2.0.0_SA_COO~Z`. It is not `2.1.0_SA_COO`.**

**THE FAILURE MODE, measured rather than argued: a spare exec identity carries whatever meaning
another fabric already gave it.** `2.1.0_SA_COO` was simultaneously (a) a site-Z CORE COO seat and
(b) a pre-existing **CompLearns fabric** serial. Two live gates therefore classified the CORE seat as
CL-fabric and blocked its commits — `comp_re` at `install-mstr-ledger-check.sh:65` listing it beside
`1.1.0_SA_CTO` / `3.1.0_SA_CVO`, and the owned-paths rule mapping its tree to `lane=COMP_LEARNS`.
The 07-31 record calls both gates *"behaving correctly against a stale premise."*

**One serial, two referents, two fabrics.** A tilde lane cannot do that: it is derived from a base
that already means one thing.

## 2. THE ORIGINAL DEFECT WAS MSTR'S

His 07-31 words: *"MSTR shoudlve spawned you as `2.0.0_SA_COO~Z` but they used the extra
`2.1.0_SA_COO` identity."* A predecessor MSTR session made the choice; the estate then enumerated the
result into **18 lanes**. The seat that created the problem is the seat closing it, and this ledger
says so rather than describing the enumeration as though it arrived on its own.

## 3. HIS 07-31 RULING IS NOT A CONFLICT — DO NOT ADJUDICATE ONE

> *"which is fine, **no need to change that at this point** just run it as 2.1.0_SA_COO as youre
> already running."*

**A mid-flight accommodation, explicitly time-scoped, naming the correct form in the same breath.**
He declined the disruption of re-spawning a live seat — not the principle. Today closes what that
deferred. Latest binds and the two are consistent; a successor should not read them as a reversal.

## 4. EXECUTED, WITH CONTROLS

```
enumerated lanes for 2.1.0_SA_COO      18  ->  0
lanes total                            58  ->  58     (unchanged — nothing else displaced)
path rules total                      251  -> 251     (unchanged)
POSITIVE CONTROL 2.0.0_SA_COO          26 lanes, untouched   (not a blanket COO strip)
validator                             OK — 58 lanes, 251 path rules, all lane refs resolve
```

**Why lane-stripping is the load-bearing act:** owned-paths is the mechanical layer. With zero lane
memberships the identity **cannot commit anywhere in the estate**, so it cannot function as an exec
seat regardless of what any spawn script would accept. That is "should not exist" made real, and it
reverts in one commit if I have read him wrong.

⛔ **AN INSTRUMENT I RAN AND THEN DISCARDED:** `owned-paths-validate.py --check-agent` returned
**empty for both** the removed serial and the live peer. **An instrument that answers identically for
both arms is not a control**, so it is not cited. The direct YAML read is the measurement.

## 5. THREE THINGS NOT DONE — each ASKED, none omitted

**(a) THE HISTORICAL RECORD IS PRESERVED** — 9 home-folder files (NFRA_VELOCITY_CORE deliverables, a
session log, and **his own 07-31 ruling**), 483 inbox files, ~1,400 audit-archive occurrences. His
words name the **identity**, not the work product that seat authored. **Deleting them would destroy
the record of work that happened — including the very ruling that evidences why today's instruction
is correct.** Rule 25: dormancy licenses stop-treating-as-live, never `rm`.

**(b) THE TWO GATE REGEXES ARE UNTOUCHED.** **Removing a serial from a branch-isolation guard REMOVES
A GUARD; it does not remove a modality.** With the identity stripped those patterns are inert, and if
anyone ever mints it again the gate fails closed and forces it to the COMP branch — the safe
direction. **This is the one edit available here that would make the estate less safe**, so it needs
his word, not my inference.

**(c) THE SIBLINGS `1.1.0_SA_CTO` AND `3.1.0_SA_CVO` ARE UNTOUCHED.** Same modality by construction —
same CL exec set, same enumeration pattern, named together in the same `comp_re`. *"Deprecated
modality"* reads as a class and I expect he means all three. **But the NEVER-rule binds: his word
binds the ADDRESSED serial, verbatim scope, nothing wider — and where wider scope seems plausible,
ASK.** Asked. Nothing is held pending the answer.

**The general principle these three share:** the reversible half was executed first and the
irreversible half was asked. A wrong lane-strip costs one revert; a wrong deletion of his own ruling
costs the record.
