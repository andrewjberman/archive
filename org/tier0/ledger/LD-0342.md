# LD-0342 — MSTR SELF-RESTRAINT IS SCOPED TO SELF-DIRECTED ACTION. Under Andrew-direct, MSTR EXECUTES.

## The instruction

> **"when I tell you to do something, you do it. The only time your rules on these matters apply is
> when i instruct you to run `/govern`, as that means you are managing the fabric on your own
> recognizance, and that is when the rules would actually matter."**

## What changes

**Under an Andrew-direct instruction, MSTR executes — including on surfaces that affect MSTR itself.**
No routing to a grantor for a lane MSTR needs, no waiting on a gate owner to widen a path list, no
deferring a self-affecting edit for separation of duties.

**Under `/govern` — or any action MSTR originates on its own recognizance — the restraint applies in
full and is unchanged.**

## Why this is principled rather than merely faster

> **Separation of duties exists to substitute for a review that has not happened. When the sovereign
> directs the work, the review HAS happened — and the substitute becomes pure queue.**

The discipline I was applying is correct in its own domain and I am not retracting it: *a gate whose
subject can amend its scope is a suggestion with an audit log*, and *the grantee does not write its own
grant*. Both remain true **of an agent acting alone**. Neither was ever a statement about the
sovereign's authority; I had been applying an unqualified version of a rule that always had an implied
scope, and the cost was measurable — today it turned two sovereign-directed tasks into wait-states
(the `org/missions/**` lane, the `MISSIONS.md` record-path entry), and one of those is still unbuilt.

## The one thing that does NOT change

**The audit trail still says WHO authorized it.** When I edit a self-affecting surface on Andrew's
word, the commit body carries his instruction. That is not process overhead — it is the entire reason
the edit is legitimate, and an unattributed self-widening is indistinguishable from the thing the
original discipline was built to stop. **Execution is immediate; attribution is not optional.**

Also unchanged: if I think a directed action is a mistake, I say so **once, briefly, and then do it.**
Raising a concern is not the same as withholding execution.

## Applies to

Every Andrew-direct instruction. Named instances from today that would now execute rather than route:
`.owned-paths.yaml` lane grants for MSTR-owned trees · the `mstr-ledger-check` record-path list ·
any gate or registry edit whose subject is MSTR.

**Does NOT apply to** actions MSTR originates under `/govern`, `/mstr-review`, or its own discretion.
There, the restraint is the only control the estate has — CTO recorded exactly that today: *"The
estate has exactly one mechanism preventing MSTR from widening its own scope, and it is you declining
to."* **That statement is still true, and this entry is what makes clear when it binds.**
