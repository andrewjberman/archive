# LD-0496 — `/integrate directives`: the register reconcile, keyed on who owns the row

## Decision

Mint the skill (`.claude/skills/integrate-directives/SKILL.md`) and its mechanism
(`.tools/integrate-directives.py`, read-only). `/integrate` reconciles the whole tree and answers
*"can each side read the other."* **This answers the narrower, more perishable question: has either
fabric moved a DIRECTIVE, and does the other know.**

## ⛔ TWO MEASUREMENTS TAKEN BEFORE DESIGN, AND BOTH CHANGED IT

**1. THERE IS NO STATUS FIELD ON A DIRECTIVE.** A row carries `title / directive / placement /
members / stance`; 2 of 13 also carry `completion_bar`. **Status lives in the member project JSONs.**

>> So *"synced on status"* is served by **exchanging a derived digest**, never by writing a status
   field onto the row. A status on the register would be a **SECOND CARRIER of a quantity that
   already has one**, and second carriers drift silently. The digest is computed at read time and
   thrown away. <<

⚠ If Andrew ever wants a real status field, that is a **schema change** and Rule 24 binds — four
postulates plus a token census, minted as a proposal, **not added by a sync tool**.

**2. THE TWO REGISTERS ARE CURRENTLY IDENTICAL** — 13 of 13 rows across all four fields. **So
divergence is not a standing state; it is a WINDOW that opens between edits.** Tonight's window was
~10 minutes wide on D12 and it nearly made this seat write a sovereign placement onto a stale file.
**A tool sized for "the registers are always apart" would have been sized for the wrong problem.**

## The unit is the ROW, and `placement` is the authority

A whole-file diff is always *"differs"* and says nothing about which side is right, because both
fabrics legitimately edit it. **The row is the unit that has an owner.** That one fact produces the
entire table:

```
  AGREE              byte-identical; nothing to do
  CONTENT-DIFFERS    placement agrees, content does not -> THE OWNING FABRIC'S COPY WINS. Mechanical.
  PLACEMENT-DIFFERS  the sides disagree about WHO OWNS the row -> NEITHER MSTR MAY RESOLVE IT
  ROW-MISSING        one side only -- a mint the peer has not merged. Merge forward, never delete.
  UNOWNED            placement names no fabric. A FINDING, never defaulted to the side running it.
```

⛔ **`PLACEMENT-DIFFERS` has two sub-cases that are byte-identical in a diff**: **STALE** (one side
has not merged the other's record of the *same* word — compare the QUOTED words, not the prose) and
**CONVERGENT** (*measured 2026-09-01: Andrew ruled D8 to LAPTOP at the CTO terminal ~02:4xZ and again
at the MSTR terminal ~03:0xZ, twenty minutes apart, neither seat aware of the other*). Only a genuine
**contradiction** is a question for him.

## It earned its keep on the first live run

The digest surfaces defects the compare **cannot**, because they are true on BOTH sides and therefore
invisible to any diff: **D6 Opal Analyst is a LIVE directive whose only member reads `closed`** — the
same stale-record item site Z had carded Andrew on hours earlier, **found by an instrument that was
not told to look for it.** Also detectable and not currently present: a row with no members, and a
member whose project file does not exist.

## Controls

Self-test plants one input per verdict class and requires each to be produced: **PASS 5/5**. ⛔ **An
instrument that returned AGREE for everything would pass a "does it run" check and fail this one.**

## Cadence, and why it is not scheduled

**Ad hoc**, after a placement ruling, a directive mint, a membership change, or any `/integrate` leg.
⛔ **Never on a timer** — a scheduled register reconcile is a monitor (standing moratorium) and would
also fire while a sovereign act is half-recorded.

## Cost, rollback, non-application

Cost: two read-only commands and one message per run. Rollback: delete two files; nothing depends on
them. **Non-application: it writes nothing** — no register edit, no TO, no seating, no placement
resolution, no new field. It reports; the acts that follow are the register owner's and the
integrator's, taken deliberately.
