# LD-0446 — MSTR may contact CRO; the work is disjoint

Full record with the verbatim, the consequence table and the cross-references:
`org/tier0_exec/0.0.0_AB_MSTR/deliverables/ANDREW_DIRECT_20260824_MSTR_MAY_CONTACT_CRO_BUT_WORK_IS_DISJOINT.md`

**Why this was asked rather than inferred, which is the transferable part.** Both available
inferences were wrong in opposite directions. Self-applying COO's and CVO's restriction would have
widened Andrew's word to a seat he did not address — the gate-manufacture class CLAUDE.md bans.
Assuming exemption would equally have been an inference, and if he had meant it fabric-wide MSTR
would have breached it while believing itself clear. **There was no safe default**, which is the
condition under which asking is correct rather than costly.

It was load-bearing rather than procedural: `3.0.0_SA_CVO` had just designated MSTR as the routing
path — *"Anything a CRO seat would need from a CVO finding routes through you"* — and that plan only
works if MSTR is not also silent.

**The distinction the answer turns on, and the one a successor is most likely to lose:** *permitted*
and *needed* are different, and he stated both. The permission is real; the need is absent. Reading
only the first half licenses exactly the traffic his second half says is pointless.

**Standing warning:** three separate scope words landed on the CRO channel in one afternoon, each
addressed to a different seat. Read the addressee before applying any of them. Collapsing them into
one fabric-wide rule is the widening this estate has paid for repeatedly, and it would land here as
either an isolated CRO or a breached 49F restriction.
