# LD-0255 -- NFRA_CMTQUE grounded-not-working + live Huntress/Rio memory-leak finding; throttle + TO-6R priority escalation (Andrew-direct)

**What.** Andrew suspected NFRA_CMTQUE (the commit-queue) isn't actually managing the fabric's commit
choke, and that this is implicated in today's Huntress-driven reboot. Investigated by reading source
(not inferring): CONFIRMED the queue is dormant fabric-wide (flag unset, no worker running -- every
commit still runs the legacy spin-poll mutex CMTQUE was built to replace), and CONFIRMED that building
CMTQUE's own worker-supervisor already produced a live 23-process fork-storm on this exact box yesterday
(LD-0248/0249). Andrew then sharpened the hypothesis to a specific mechanism -- Huntress's own event-
processing falling behind git-process-creation volume, manifesting as a memory leak, not observed pre-
CMTQUE. Live-instrumented Rio.exe (Huntress's core process): **confirmed an active leak** -- two
~2-minute-apart samples showed +182 handles / +66MB (~90 handles/min, ~34MB/min), on an instance only
90 minutes old (started at today's post-reboot boot) already at 1.3GB / 4,000+ handles vs a fresh
baseline of 12MB / 145 handles.

**Honest caveat.** The CPU-spike/fork-failure symptom predates CMTQUE (diagnosed 2026-07-01). The
specific fast-memory-leak signature may be genuinely new, but there's no pre-CMTQUE Rio memory baseline
to prove causation -- CORRELATED and mechanistically consistent, not independently CONFIRMED-CAUSAL at
the Huntress-internals level (closed-source agent).

**Andrew's decisions (via AskUserQuestion), executed:**
1. **Throttle concurrency now** -- directed COO: no new spawn/dispatch bursts, stagger new work, pull
   back to lane-pausing rather than adding load, treat the 20-agent cap tighter than usual given this
   box's 8 logical CPUs. COO ACK'd and already deferred one would-be new DS respawn in compliance.
2. **Escalate NFRA_CMTQUE.TO-6R priority** -- directed COO to move the load-test + phased cutover above
   the rest of the backlog; MSTR will proactively name the quiet window (COO signals when active-
   dispatch volume drops) rather than wait for one to occur organically. Caveat added: a load-test
   itself briefly adds forking burst, so "quiet" means genuinely low session-count, not just
   no-new-dispatches; if Rio's growth doesn't level off on its own, IT-side Huntress remediation may be
   a needed parallel track, outside fabric authority to act on unilaterally.

**Downstream.** COO driving both; MSTR watching for COO's quiet-window signal to authorize TO-6R.
Entry closes (or gets a follow-on) when TO-6R lands or the Rio leak trend is independently re-assessed.
