# LD-0477 — Controlled wind-down of the local fabric for a device reboot

## The order

Andrew, verbatim at the MSTR terminal 2026-08-29 21:23Z:

> "YES I WANT A CONTROLLED WIND DOWN OF THE LOCAL FABRIC SO THAT WE CAN REBOOT THE DEVICE
> I NEED YOU TO PICK OUT THE CTO LEADERS WHO ARE STILL ONLINE TO EFFECTIVELY DOCUMENT THE STATE OF
> THEIR RESPECTIVE EFFORTS SO THEY CAN BE INSTANTIATED AND RESTART THEIR LOOPS"

**This is a sovereign instruction and it is the one condition under which MSTR winds a fabric down** —
the standing rule is NEVER self-wind-down.

## The sequence, and why it is in this order

1. **Every seat stops taking new work and COMMITS.**
2. **The leader seats write a reinstantiation handoff** (`/handoff save` — persists AND commits).
3. **MSTR runs the FINAL integration window** and pushes everything to origin.
4. **MSTR winds the seats down.**
5. **MSTR reports the device safe to reboot.**

⛔ **COMMIT COMES FIRST BECAUSE IT IS THE ONLY IRREVERSIBLE THING IN THE SEQUENCE.** A local commit
survives a reboot; a dirty working tree does not. At the moment the order was issued the tree carried
**282 dirty files**. Everything else here — killing seats, losing threads, dropping sessions — is
recoverable by respawn. Uncommitted bytes are not.

⭐ **AND THE PUSH IS NOT OPTIONAL EITHER, for a different reason:** a local commit survives the reboot
but is invisible to the other fabric and to any successor that clones. Publication is what makes the
work exist for anyone but this machine.

## Who was named, and who was already gone

Live at 21:2xZ, measured on **process truth** (claude.exe command-line serial token + codex heartbeat
sidecars), not the broker roster: **10 claude + 3 codex.**

**Leaders instructed to write handoffs:** `1.0.0_SA_CTO`, `1.0.0_SA_CTO~X`, `2.0.0_SA_COO~X`,
`3.0.0_SA_CVO~X`, `4.0.0_SA_CRO~X`, `4.0.0_SA_CRO~Y`.

⚠ **BASE `2.0.0_SA_COO` AND BASE `3.0.0_SA_CVO` HAD ALREADY ENDED BEFORE THE ORDER.** They are not in
the list because they are gone, and **anything they held uncommitted was already at risk when Andrew
spoke.** Named explicitly rather than quietly omitted — a roster that silently drops the departed reads
as complete.

⛔ **CODEX SEATS LOSE EVERYTHING BUT THEIR COMMITS.** Their threads and context do not survive the
reboot, and their outbound carries no broker row so their acknowledgements cannot be verified by the
usual instrument. They were told to prioritise the handoff over all other work, and their departure is
measured rather than awaited.

## What the reboot fixes for free, recorded so nobody chases it afterwards

- **Hand-started process reap obligations become MOOT** — the pids die with the machine.
- **`1.0.0_SA_CTO`'s liveness `os.replace` retry finally reaches memory.** It landed ~20:15Z, but at
  21:0xZ all three heartbeat sidecars predated it and were executing pre-fix bytes: correct, committed,
  pushed, deployed, and protecting nothing. **A restart is what makes that fix real.**

## What does NOT survive and must be carried by the handoffs

Every seat's in-session reasoning, its place in its loop, and anything RESERVED or HELD that a fresh
seat would not know to keep waiting on. **That is the whole reason Andrew asked for the documents
rather than just a shutdown** — the fabric's restart map is the deliverable, not the shutdown itself.
