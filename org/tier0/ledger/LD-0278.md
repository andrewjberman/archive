# LD-0278 — A negative governance claim may not be asserted from a pinned window

## The decision

Amend `.claude/skills/mstr-review/SKILL.md` Step 0 to treat **positive and negative findings
asymmetrically**, and to forbid asserting a behavioural absence from a window whose END precedes the
review's own write-time.

## Why (the incident, which is mine)

RV-0046 reported, verbatim:

> *"MSTR-self overrides in-window: 0. RV-0043's carry-forward F-002 (recurring MSTR BASE_EXEC
> owned-paths override, 11x and counting) did **not** recur this window… the behaviour has stopped."*

It had not stopped. Two MSTR-self `OVERRIDE` lines sit in the commit-scope audit log at
`2026-07-10T02:41:45Z` and `02:44:24Z` — **56 minutes after RV-0046's pinned END of `01:45:47Z`** — and
both are self-labelled, by MSTR, as *"F-002 recurrence 12"*.

**The substance of those overrides is defensible and is not the finding.** CTO authored a fabric-wide
reachability fix across 16 lanes, refused `--force` on separation-of-duties grounds, and MSTR landed it
under an attributed, richly-reasoned, Andrew-routed override. That is the system working.

**The finding is the measurement.** RV-0046 pinned its END *deliberately*, reasoning that "the fabric was
committing live throughout this review." That reasoning is sound for deciding which records to **include**.
It is exactly wrong for concluding an **absence** — and the two were never distinguished.

> A window pinned before the present cannot support a claim about the present. A `0` read from it is not a
> measurement of the behaviour; it is a measurement of the boundary.

## The class, and why the existing rules could not catch it

This is the RV-0026 (tail-N) / RV-0028 (calendar-day) boundary-error class — **recurring inside the review
that cites it.** Step 0's window-pinning discipline was followed to the letter: author-timestamps, explicit
`awk` ranges, no date-prefix grep, both boundaries recorded as shas.

Every rule was obeyed and the error happened anyway, because **the rules govern how you pin a window, not
what you may conclude from one.** A rule about retrieval cannot catch an error of inference.

It is also the week's dominant shape in a new costume. The fabric has spent a month cataloguing instruments
whose output is invariant under the truth. This is an instrument whose output is invariant under the truth
*in one direction only*: a pinned window can only ever **under**-report a behaviour, so it is safe for
positives and structurally unsafe for negatives. Nobody had noticed the asymmetry because nobody had asked
the window to prove a negative before.

## The amendment (as landed)

- **POSITIVE findings** — a record exists, an override fired, a commit landed — remain safe to assert from
  the pinned window. Under-counting a positive still leaves a true positive.
- **NEGATIVE findings** — a behaviour "stopped", a count of zero, an absence, a "did not recur" — must be
  **re-measured at write-time against HEAD**, or stated as what they actually are: *"not observed as of
  `<END>`"*. Never as *"stopped."*
- And the sentence that generalises it: **the liveness that justifies the pin is the same liveness that
  invalidates the zero.**

## Rollback

`git revert` the SKILL.md hunk. Nothing else is touched.
