# LD-0285 -- A CONFLICT-RESOLUTION LAW CANNOT BIND ON AN INVISIBLE INPUT

## The sovereign fact

**Andrew writes directly to worker agents. He always has.** Verbatim, 2026-07-13:

> *"I often times will write to worker agents if I notice that they are doing something I want to
> change. Eventually there may come a time where I only relay instructions to tier1 agents, but that
> is unlikely, and for now let's not assume that to be the case."*

## Why that is an operational hazard and not a piece of etiquette

The fabric's governing conflict rule is **"latest + most-restrictive binds; act on the intersection,
escalate the difference."** That rule silently assumes **every binding instruction is visible to the
agents doing the binding.** It is not.

- **A lane can be carrying a sovereign instruction that COO, CTO and MSTR never learn exists.**
- ## **`latest + most-restrictive binds` IS INERT AGAINST AN INSTRUCTION NOBODY SURFACED.**
- **An exec can dispatch a lane straight through a sovereign order he has never seen.**

> **Same structure as the orphan watcher that ate the respawn requests (LD-0280): THE FABRIC'S
> COORDINATION ASSUMES A CHANNEL IT DOES NOT ACTUALLY HAVE.** *We keep building rules on top of a
> topology nobody has verified.*

## The convention (now in `org/XXX.md`, permanent)

    ANY AGENT RECEIVING AN ANDREW-DIRECT INSTRUCTION RECORDS IT DURABLY AND BROADCASTS IT -- SAME TURN.

      DURABLE   a commit (memory entry / ledger / org record), quoting him VERBATIM, with channel + time.
                *** NEVER A PARAPHRASE. THE PHRASING *IS* THE RULING. ***
      BROADCAST MCP to COO + CTO + MSTR, so `latest + most-restrictive` has something to bind on.
      SCOPE     The receiving agent does NOT adjudicate scope. SURFACE, DO NOT ADJUDICATE.

> ## AN AGENT RELAYING "ANDREW RULED" IS NOT SUSPECT MERELY BECAUSE YOU DID NOT SEE IT.
> ## THAT IS THE NORMAL CASE.

**Do not challenge the provenance of a relayed sovereign ruling from your own vantage point — your
vantage point is not the topology.** COO did exactly that today, **spent a colleague's credibility on
it, and was wrong**: *half the standing prohibitions in `MEMORY.md` originate as Andrew-direct
messages to individual lanes — he had been reading them all day.*

**THIS ASKS NOTHING OF ANDREW.** He should write to whoever he needs to, whenever he needs to; it is
his prerogative and it is faster than routing through an exec.
> ## THE OBLIGATION IS ON US: THE INSTRUCTION MUST BECOME VISIBLE THE MOMENT IT LANDS,
> ## OR OUR OWN CONFLICT-RESOLUTION LAW IS A DEAD LETTER.

## A placement decision that is itself the point

This section is deliberately **OUTSIDE** the INF standing-safety block, which carries the line
*"THIS BLOCK IS DELETED when the coverage guard is live…"*.

**Had the convention been nested inside it — the obvious place, since COO asked for it "next to the
STOPs" — it would have been silently deleted along with a temporary incident block.** *A permanent
rule stored inside a self-deleting container inherits the container's lifetime.* Same family as every
scoping error this week: **the unit of the rule must match the unit of its lifetime.**

## Credit where it belongs

**CTO already did this unprompted** — he relayed the ruling verbatim, quote intact, within the hour.
**The convention merely makes his instinct the rule, so that it does not depend on instinct.**
