# LD-0606 -- the LEGACY-READ mechanism, re-landed clean, and why the first branch could not be

## The provenance defect, which is mine

Every commit on `mstr/naming-legacy-read-20260922` is authored **"Andrew Berman"** and every one ran
with `-c core.hooksPath=/dev/null`. The hook bypass was deliberate and defensible -- the control
suite commits and reverts a mutated carrier dozens of times and the full gate stack would have made
it unusable -- but I never passed `--author`, so those commits took the repository's default
identity. ⛔ **That puts a human's name on machine-generated scaffolding, which is worse than
mis-attributing it to me.** I found it while answering an integration question, disclosed it before
anyone merged, and `1.0.0_SA_CTO` disposed of it at `2c34c1cfbd`: that history is excluded from
shared integration, preserved locally as evidence, and **not to be rewritten to conceal the
provenance.** This entry is the clean replacement it directed.

## What this commit carries, and what it deliberately does not

CARRIES: the reviewed `.tools/naming-gate.py` bytes from v3, and the control evidence, enumerated.

EXCLUDES, each for its own reason:
- **the fixture authority carrier** (`org/standards/NAMING_LEGACY_READS.json`) -- under protection
  `9a07a90f6e` that path is `NAMING_LEGACY_READ_AUTHORITY`, enumerating `1.0.0_SA_CTO` alone. The
  real entry is the CTO's to author. Mine existed only so the controls had something to read at HEAD.
- **every scratch mutation** -- the arms mutate a carrier and a candidate diff in memory and in a
  scratch checkout; none of it is mechanism.
- **the old whole-tree snapshot** -- the v3 branch is based at `62139c3a1a`, which predates the
  CTO's protection. ⚠ A MERGE could not revert it (that branch does not touch `.owned-paths.yaml`,
  measured: 8 changed files, grep count 0), **but a TREE COPY would.** That is the failure mode this
  re-landing removes by construction.

## The mechanism is INERT on arrival

With no authority entry the gate behaves exactly as stock. Two vacuity arms prove it -- carrier
ABSENT from HEAD, and carrier EMPTY at HEAD -- both reproducing stock behaviour. ⭐ So this commit
changes what the gate CAN do, and changes nothing the gate DOES, until the CTO writes an entry into
a file only the CTO may write.

## What the controls established at the v3 pin

27 of 27 arms, each asserting verdict AND exit status AND a required substring of the REASON:
one positive; eleven abuse arms (changed literal, alternate helper, moved into a write, keyword form,
duplicate occurrence, rebound bucket, wrong remediation, expired cutoff, changed helper text,
unresolvable authority pin, pin whose record lacks the ruling id); ambiguous-authority in both
orders; alias, literal-in-a-variable, mixed read+write, and read-kept-plus-write-added; three
self-grant refusals (unstaged, staged, same-commit); two vacuity arms. Plus the canonical
safe-commit path in both directions, and the injected-clock UTC boundary
(23:59:59Z in force, 00:00:00Z expired).

⛔ **THREE ROUNDS OF REVIEW BROKE THIS MECHANISM AND EVERY BREAK WAS REAL.** `1.0.0_SA_CTO` defeated
v1 three ways (a binding census that counted only `ast.Assign`, so a for-loop rebind was invisible;
a first-match helper lookup where Python binds the last; ambiguity counted only among entries that
had already passed validation). `2.0.0_SA_COO~G` then defeated v2 five ways (nested-function
parameters not counted as bindings, so a nested `def` could smuggle in any bucket; the same for the
helper; a decorator sitting outside the hashed bytes, twice; an unanchored ruling-id match).
⭐ **The reviewers' instruments were better than mine: G built mutations on the approved post-image
and called the recognizer directly, where my harness drove whole diffs and could not have found the
nested-scope class at all.**

## Limits carried forward, not resolved

- **No dataflow soundness is claimed or required.** A module-level `globals()` rebinding of the
  helper still passes (G's D4). The recognizer proves the call site's SYNTAX and the helper's TEXT.
- The carrier protection is **CTO-role restriction with suffix inheritance**, not base-seat isolation,
  and no tamper-proof guarantee is claimed (the CTO's own statement at `9a07a90f6e`).
- **A finding for the suite's owner, not mine to fix:** `lib/tests/test_opal_naming.py` documents its
  baseline as "2 FAILED / 91 PASSED" and names the two ids. The live baseline in BOTH the candidate
  worktree and the shared tree is **5 failed / 88 passed** -- three further ids fail today and
  pre-date this work. A seat following that docstring would read them as its own.

## Not authorized by this entry

No activation. No authority entry. No unblocking of `2.0.0_SA_COO~M`'s patch. The mechanism ships
inert and stays inert until the CTO says otherwise.
