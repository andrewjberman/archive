## THE DETERMINISTIC RULE

> For a given business date, collect ALL "Short Sale Avail File" emails (sender `SmartLoan9990@fisglobal.com`, subject `Short Sale Avail File`) **received on that date**, and sort by **send time (`receivedDateTime`) ascending**:
> - **FIRST (earliest)** → rename ` YYYYMMDD PRELIM`
> - **SECOND (latest)** → rename ` YYYYMMDD`
>
> The discriminator is **arrival/sent-time ORDER within the date** — NOT subject, NOT body (identical), NOT a hardcoded clock cutoff.

## WHY arrival-ORDER, not a clock-cutoff or a text-marker

- **Text marker RULED OUT (grounded):** the original subject is exactly `Short Sale Avail File` in 100% of samples (no PRELIM/final), and the body is identical boilerplate (`Please find attached Short Sale Avail File for SmartLoan`). Nothing in subject or body distinguishes the two. So the rule CANNOT key on text.
- **Hardcoded clock-cutoff RULED OUT (would misfire):** the first-email time drifts (5:01–5:24 AM) and there's at least one same-subject email at **2026-04-01 10:23 AM** with the second-email at 7:01–7:03 AM — a fixed "before 6 AM = prelim" cutoff would misclassify the drift + the Apr-1 outlier. **Order-within-the-date is robust to time drift; a clock cutoff is not.** (The ~5 AM / ~7 AM split is corroborating context, NOT the rule.)
- **Arrival-order is deterministic + drift-proof:** sort the date's emails by `receivedDateTime`; position 1 = PRELIM, position 2 = final.

## EDGE CASES / CAVEATS (must be handled by step-1 — flag to CTO + Yang)

1. **Single-email days.** Before ~2026-03-16 there was only ONE email/day (~5 AM); occasional anomalies persist (e.g., **2026-04-01** showed a single 10:23 AM send in the sample window). **Step-1 needs a policy when only one email has arrived for the date:** (a) treat the lone email as the **final**, OR (b) wait for the second until a daily cutoff, then treat the lone one as final. **Recommend (b) with a cutoff (~9–10 AM ET): if a second arrives by the cutoff, first=PRELIM/second=final; if only one by the cutoff, treat it as final.** Operational decision — surface to Yang.
2. **Scope to the SAME business date.** Sort by date FIRST, then within-date by time — do not just take "the two most recent emails" (across a weekend that pairs Fri+Mon).
3. **Production reads ORIGINAL emails, not Yang's forwards.** This sample is Yang's `FW:` (all forwarded 2026-06-26 18:0X Z, so the forward `receivedDateTime` is useless — the original send-time is in the body's `Sent:` line). In production, step-1 reads the LIVE FIS emails delivered to the intake mailbox, where the email's own `receivedDateTime` IS the ~5 AM / ~7 AM FIS send-time → the rule applies directly to `receivedDateTime`, no body-parsing needed.
4. **Optional secondary confirm (not required):** the rule does NOT need the attachment. IF the step-1 build adds attachment-fetch and the attached filename happens to encode prelim/final or a timestamp, use it as a belt-and-suspenders confirm of the time-order — but time-order alone is the deterministic rule. (The gateway's current read surface does not expose attachment names; not a blocker for S0.3.)
