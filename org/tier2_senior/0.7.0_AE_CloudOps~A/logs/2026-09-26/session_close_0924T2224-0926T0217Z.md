## The two criteria that went red after a landed act, and why neither was rolled back

This is the substance of the session; both are the same shape and both were decided by measurement.

**ro-7 AC2 (16 of 17).** The halves name a rollback as the trigger for a post-apply criterion failure. Before
taking it I asked whether it could work: `git show 19a22f2f51:lib/research/print/render_pack.py` contains
`"Description: ABSENT"` **0 times**, against 1 at HEAD (positive control). The artifact a rollback restores is
structurally incapable of covering the 17th absence, so AC2 was ALREADY failing on the served plane before my
deploy and the rollback could only have removed ro-1's 165-figure export and the 16 absences that matched.
Held L3 on a named gate, filed row `Z-S271`. The issuer cut the content fix (`f3de463222`), both halves signed
amendment B2, and the second apply read 17 of 17.

**rr-3 AC2 (CAPTURE_FAILED 1 of 95).** Same question. The failure is raised at
`crosswalk.harvester_key` / `scoped_amgt.resolve_identity`; `inspect.getsource` over `resolve_identity` finds
NO network token -- it is a local crosswalk read -- so the TypeError precedes any call reaching the deployed
function and re-pointing the artifact cannot mint an identity. The failing member is folder `AIA Group`
(harvester_key `security-a49e6fe67bebe3a9`), absent from the 89-name snapshot and from the crosswalk entirely,
added to the live library AFTER the order recorded N as 94. Held L2, filed row `Z-S284`. The issuer pinned the
population (amendment 1 @`a2072789f1`) and carried AIA Group to round 44; its reply: *"Your hold was the right
call."*

**The generalization, and it is the one worth keeping:** a named rollback in a signed sign-off proves the act
is SAFE to reverse; it never proves reversing it REPAIRS the failure that fired it. Those are two claims. The
discriminator is cheap -- was the criterion passing before the act, and can the restored artifact satisfy it at
all -- and both times the answer made the rollback the wrong act.
