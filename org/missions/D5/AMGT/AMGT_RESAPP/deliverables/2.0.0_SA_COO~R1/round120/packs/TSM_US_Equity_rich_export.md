## Management/Board

Management and capital allocation: The management team is solid, and the company consistently executes near flawlessly. Prior to his appointment in 2018, CEO C.C. Wei was president and co-CEO from 2013, and co-COO for the year prior. From 2009 to 2012, he led business development, and before that he was president of mainstream technology. Before joining TSMC in 1998, he held senior positions at Chartered Semiconductor and ST Microelectronics, and prior to that, he was a member of the technical staff at Texas Instruments in the R&D organization. Wei was awarded the Semiconductor Industry Association’s Robert N. Noyce Award in 2025 (this is the semiconductor industry’s highest honor). Outside of Wei, most other executives have been at TSMC for decades. They have a strong engineering-first culture and proven ability to execute at massive scale. On capital allocation, priorities include reinvesting back into the business, with cap ex dedicated to building out production capacity, and returning excess capital to shareholders with a bias toward dividends. While cap ex spend can fluctuate year to year, it is consistently high on an absolute basis ($41bn in 2025, expected to be $56bn in 2026) and as a percent of total sales (typically in the mid-30s to low-40s range). Cap ex spend topped 50% of total sales just five years ago, in 2021. TSMC has returned more than $117bn USD in dividends to shareholders since 1994, and the current dividend yield is 1.1%. They’ve repurchased $3.2bn in shares over that same time frame. TSMC has historically not been acquisitive.
