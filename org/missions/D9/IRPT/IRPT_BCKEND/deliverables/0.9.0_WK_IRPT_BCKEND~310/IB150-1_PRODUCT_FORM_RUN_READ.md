## The trade, plainly
40 trading days (about two calendar months), the day graded included. Over the 300 securities of the nine pairs outside the first witness with bars and the 85 graded days, the rule takes, by window (securities taken on one or more days / security-days un-graded beyond the guard / securities graded on some days and taken on others / account-days no longer freed): 10: 30 / 233 / 27 / 156; 20: 22 / 255 / 18 / 241; 40: 14 / 272 / 8 / 287; 60: 14 / 325 / 7 / 341; 85: 14 / 372 / 7 / 374. A short window lets a spell of a few repeated bars take a security that otherwise moves: 27 and 18 securities change verdict inside the run at 10 and 20 days. From 40 days the set of securities taken stops growing (14 at 40, 14 at 60, 14 at 85) and the number that change verdict settles (8 at 40; 7 at 60; 7 at 85), while a longer window costs more freed account-days (287 at 40, 341 at 60, 374 at 85) and un-grades more security-days for want of history (54, 93, 134). 40 is the shortest window at which the verdicts have settled. THE TRADE, plainly: a fixed window is reproducible (the same security-day, the same grade, whatever the run) but it is not the whole-run reading v0.43 wrote. The seven securities of IB-pr-452 not graded on any day cost 33 account-days and 1 account; the 40-day window takes those seven and 7 more, each on part of the run, and costs 287 account-days and 2 accounts. The row-days un-graded by the rule on the days it does not grade are most on EIBIX, VMLUX, OLCYX (3328, 342, 147 row-days): securities near the one-half edge are un-graded on the days a window finds more than half repeated and graded on the others.

## Not established
- That 40 trading days is the right window: several were measured and one is proposed; the CTO writes the contract.  The short-history floor (half the window) is chosen, not derived.
- Why a security's bar repeats (a stale feed, a fund that does not move, a late publication): the counts cannot tell them apart, and the rule does not try.
- That the securities the rule takes beyond IB-pr-452's seven are wrongly graded by the second witness: they repeat on about half their days and are taken on part of the run; nothing here says their moved bars are dated wrong.
- That a freed account-day's claim would be correct: freed account-days are a count of account-days no witness is barred from grading, not a check that the claim is right.
- The distribution on any day but the last graded day (2026-10-09).
- The overlap securities' sizing: they are counted in tenths only; the first witness grades them and the rule would not touch a claim of the first witness.
- Anything outside the 85 graded days, the 878 tickers asked, or the securities with no ticker, no type, mixed types or another type (not graded, not counted in the tenths).
- The 7 tickers for which the reader returned nothing: their rows block their account-days (the module gives no grade without a bar).
- Whether the sizing under the rule would be the sizing a producer reads: no producer, schema or schedule exists; nothing is served and no claim is re-graded in the product.
