## Why it gave pause

### Ambiguity

Two seats write code that this one function runs: `~F2` the transaction stage, and `~D2` the filled stage. The failing line sits in the filled stage, but `~F2` read it first, from the transaction fire.

### Out of pattern

Normally the seat that reads a fault fixes it. Here the reader's own stage is unaffected, and the fix is an IAM change on a role whose root another seat owns.

### Other solutions

- **(b) `~F2` adds the statement inside its transaction pair.** This is worse: it widens a signed pair beyond its own stage, and it makes two writers of one policy in one week.
- **(c) Remove the catch-up listing and owe only the written day by design.** This is worse: a missed fire would never be recovered.
- **(d) Leave it INERT.** This is acceptable short-term, because the returns are unaffected. But a missed fire would silently leave a hole in the filled table.
