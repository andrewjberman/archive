## CE FINDINGS AGAINST THE PROPOSED SET

### 1. NO MEMBER FOR "THE NEED WAS MET ANOTHER WAY" -- and the estate has a RATIFIED vocabulary for exactly this distinction
The proposed four cover *the client withdrew* (`withdrawn`), *time ran out* (`expired`), and
*Opal declines* (`out_of_scope`, `duplicate`). **Nothing covers: the request was satisfied,
just not through this ticket** -- answered in the Teams thread, resolved by an adjacent
ticket, the artifact appeared by another route. On live-beta traffic where the same analyst
answers in-channel and on the board, this is not an edge case.
**The estate already ruled this vocabulary, Andrew-direct 2026-08-07** (`org/XXX.md:520`,
`org/ref/TASK_ORDER_PROTOCOL.md:290-291`): **`superseded` = the NEED WAS MET ANOTHER WAY**
vs **`cancelled` = the NEED WENT AWAY**. The proposed `withdrawn` is the `cancelled` half;
the `superseded` half has no member.
**And the reason the estate keeps them distinct is LOAD-BEARING HERE SPECIFICALLY** --
`TASK_ORDER_PROTOCOL.md:297`, verbatim: *"KEEP THE TWO DISTINCT -- they differ on RE-OPEN,
which is the only time it matters. `superseded` means the outcome was achieved; re-opening
asks 'is the replacement still standing?' `cancelled` means the outcome is unwanted;
re-opening needs a FRESH AUTHORIZATION. Collapsing them loses which question to ask."*
**`1.0.0_SA_CTO~Z` sec3 rules REOPEN into this very schema as a requester-class MATRIX
action.** So the one condition under which the distinction matters is not hypothetical here
-- it is being built in the same wave. Build-ladder rung 2 also applies: this is a ratified
in-estate term, and re-coining beside it is the duplicate Rule 24's granularity amendment
names.

### 2. THE SELF-NAMED UNCOVERED MEMBER SPLITS IN TWO, AND ONLY ONE IS TERMINAL
`1.0.0_SA_CTO~Z` names it: *"Opal did the work and the requester rejects the outcome."*
That is **two different things on two different axes**:
- **the requester rejects and wants more** -> **NOT TERMINAL.** It is the REOPEN trigger,
  and it belongs on the reopen path, not on a terminal-reason axis.
- **the requester rejects and Opal will not do more** -> terminal, and genuinely uncovered
  (a `disputed`-class reason: work delivered, outcome not accepted, nobody continuing).
**Adding the undifferentiated member to `closure_reason` would put a non-terminal state on a
terminal axis** -- the same intent/grain flattening the two-field split was ruled to prevent,
arriving through the back door as a "known gap" rather than as a value. Naming the split is
the whole remedy; which half to adopt is the owner's.

### 3. THE DECLARED LAYER IS NOT STATED, AND CE IS EVALUATED AGAINST IT
The widened Rule 24 evaluates collective-exhaustiveness **against the declared layer**, so
the record must declare `closure_reason`'s layer before its CE check means anything. It is
not obvious which of these is intended, and they enumerate differently:
*(a)* every reason a ticket reaches the non-completed terminal state; *(b)* every reason a
ticket ends **without the requester getting what they asked for** (excludes `superseded`,
which delivered the outcome elsewhere); *(c)* every reason **Opal stopped working it**.
My findings above are written against **(a)**. Under (b), finding 1 changes shape entirely.
**Declare it first; the layer choice decides the member list, not the other way round.**

### 4. THE POINTER REQUIREMENT COMES WITH THE VOCABULARY, AND THE CLIENT VIEW SHARPENS IT
`org/XXX.md:520`, verbatim: **"a terminal status WITHOUT ITS POINTER is the rot with a tidier
label."** The ratified terms carry mandatory pointers -- `superseded` names what superseded
it, `cancelled` cites the ruling. **`duplicate` without a pointer to the canonical ticket is
the same rot**, and W-C makes it worse than internal rot: a client sees their request marked
duplicate **with no path to where it actually lives**. Recommend the record make the pointer
a FIELD REQUIREMENT per value, not a convention -- and that the client projection carry the
pointer for any reason that has one.

### 5. INTERNAL-RECORDED IS NOT CLIENT-RENDERED (the P0's shape, one layer over)
The split gives the internal record a correct home. **It does not by itself decide what the
client sees**, and the original defect was client-facing: the client's tabs are
Open / In Progress / Completed. A second terminal status value has no tab. If it renders
under "Completed", a withdrawn or out-of-scope ticket still reads to the client as resolved
-- **the exact defect, now with better internal data behind it.** The tab mapping per
terminal value (and which values show no AI summary) is a required part of this ruling, not
a W-C detail. Same shape as the P0: row-scoped was not field-scoped; internally-recorded is
not client-rendered.
