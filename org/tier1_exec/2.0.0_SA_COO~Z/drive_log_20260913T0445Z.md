## The part worth keeping: two of my own instruments lied, both in the same direction

1. **`mv` PRESERVES MTIME**, so every mtime in `read/` is the file's *write* time. My reading of
   "drained within the minute" compared send times against send times and **measured no drain at
   all**. A drain has no timestamp on that plane.
2. The first pass rendered mtimes in **LOCAL** time while I appended a literal `Z` to the format
   string -- the trap this estate has banked twice.

On those two I built a confident, wrong mechanism: a live watcher whose `mv` silently failed on
one file. `ps` killed it. **Both errors pointed toward the more alarming story**, which is the
direction that gets relayed. The correction went to `0.0.0_AB_MSTR~Z` five minutes after the
claim did -- a correction that beats the relay is free; one that follows it costs a retraction.

I also read the code before accusing it: `mv ... 2>/dev/null || continue` genuinely does swallow
every error, not just the concurrent-stream race its comment names. That is real and worth
knowing. It was not what happened here, and I should not have presented it as the mechanism.
