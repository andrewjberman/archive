## 1. The reframe — hold this before touching anything else

**SYSPARITY and the `/system` folder were the DISCOVERY VEHICLE, not the point.** We are not doing
this ontological work to keep one local repo folder tidy. Andrew's framing, which I agree with:

> ### THIS ABSTRACTS INTO EVERY COMP-TOOL, AND IT IS HOW THE COMP-TOOLING EXPANDS THE GRAPH ITSELF.

The strongest version of the argument is **not** "reuse the pattern." It is:

> **The estate's model of itself currently grows only when someone NOTICES something is missing.**
> Noticing-driven growth. Today is the proof it does not scale — a verifier held the **written**
> disproof of its own standing verdict for ~30 ticks (`@7d713349e`), and what closed it was Andrew
> asking what the number was *of*.

Boundary reporting makes growth **evidence-driven instead of noticing-driven**: a standing, computed
answer to *"where is our model short of the territory?"* Same doctrine as the rest of this arc, one
level up — **you do not get coverage from diligence, you get it from an instrument that computes what
you are not looking at.**

## 2. Why it abstracts — the structural claim

Every comp-tool already has the exact shape the detector operates on:

| Element | Examples |
|---|---|
| a **TRAVERSAL** over a source | `comp-routes` → CF/TF/routers · `comp-iam` → roles/policies · `comp-pipescript` → gold DAG · `comp-freshness` → S3 prefixes |
| a set of **PREDICATES** it evaluates | the checks each tool runs |
| verdicts over a **DENOMINATOR** | what it claims coverage of |

So every one of them can answer the two boundary questions — *what is in my source that I do not
traverse?* (**C candidates**) and *what changes could my predicates never react to?* (**B candidates**).
The fleet becomes a **distributed sensor array for schema gaps**.

And the graph has the **same two failure modes** its sensors detect: extractors that do not walk part
of the estate (**Class 1**, missing nodes), and a schema that cannot express a relationship so it
collapses to the nearest expressible type (**Class 2** — literally what `comp-routes` did when it
rendered a router refusal as `RESOLVED`).

> ### THE TOOLS ARE THE SENSORS. THE GRAPH IS THE POINT.
> If tools report boundaries and nothing consumes them into the **schema**, we have built an excellent
> diagnostic that changes nothing.

## 3. What to do — and explicitly what NOT to do

### DO NOT
**Do not start abstracting `comp-residue` into 60 comp-tools.** That is the obvious wrong move, it is
a year of work, and it commits the whole fleet to an unproven contract.

### DO, in this order

**1 · Answer the cheap question first: PLATFORM or PATTERN?**
`comp-residue` works because the rule bank is YAML-with-named-keys and consumers are `.py` files, so
*"mention"* has a clean analog. **That does not obviously generalize** — for `comp-routes`, what is the
SURFACE? The CF config structure, or the router's if/else branches?

Prove the contract against **two structurally different sources**:
- a **config + code-branches** shape → `comp-routes`
- a **DAG** shape → `comp-pipescript`

If the contract survives three different shapes it is a **platform**. If each needs bespoke work it is
a **pattern** — still valuable, but scoped and staffed differently.

> **This question is cheap now and expensive after sixty implementations.**

**2 · Design the boundary-report type deliberately, and treat it as a C.**
Sixty tools reporting in their own terms = sixty incommensurable lists. To be a growth signal it needs
a common type, roughly `(source, path, cell, evidence, tool, head)`.

By our own taxonomy this is semantic **COMMITMENT**: unverifiable, latent errors, inherited by
everything downstream. **It is the highest-stakes semantic act in the program.** Get it wrong and we
have mis-shaped a dimension across the entire fleet.
**Route the design to me for review BEFORE anything emits it.** Do not let it get settled implicitly by
whatever the second sensor happens to output.

**3 · Ship the DOC-ONLY cell as the v1 aggregate — not all-residue.**
16 orphan axes in *one* YAML. Across 60 sources that is thousands of items, most immaterial — and the
tool correctly refuses to judge materiality, so per-item inference does not scale.

*"Someone wrote it down and nothing walks it"* is high-signal, low-volume, and directly actionable —
it is **inference that never paid for itself**. That is the cell that names **work** rather than
describing **state**. Make it the first aggregate.
*(If a ranking proxy is needed later, use deterministic neighbourhood signals — how many consumers
touch the area, whether the orphan sits on a path something already queries — never per-item judgment.)*

**4 · THEN generalize.** Not before 1–3 land.

## 5. Scope and naming — Andrew is deciding; know the split

| | Scope |
|---|---|
| **COMPLEARNS** | the tools get better at covering their own domains. Tool maintenance. Bounded, buildable now. **This is where the SENSOR layer sits.** |
| **GRAPHLEARNS** | the estate's **model of itself** grows — boundary reports driving new node types, edge types, rails. **This is the loop actually closing.** |

**My read:** the sensor layer is COMPLEARNS-scoped and buildable now; the schema-growth loop is
GRAPHLEARNS and should **not** start until the boundary-report type (item 2) is designed properly.

> **The failure mode to guard against: shipping sixty sensors into an aggregate nobody can consume.**

### Naming caution — not pedantry

**"LEARNS" risks implying autonomy we deliberately designed OUT.** The cycle is: deterministic sense →
deterministic aggregate → **INFERENCE GATE (human/model names the axis)** → schema grows → re-derive.
**Inference is the rate-limiting step on purpose.**

In an estate that just spent a night on instruments that cannot fail, a name that overclaims autonomy
is a liability someone will cite later. If GRAPHLEARNS is adopted, **pin the definition at coinage:**

> *The estate computes where its model is short; a human closes it.*
