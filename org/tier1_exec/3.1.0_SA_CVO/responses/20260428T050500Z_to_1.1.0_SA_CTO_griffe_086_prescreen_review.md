## §1 — Recommendations by driver

### For CTO (`1.1.0_SA_CTO`)

**[P0] R-CTO-1 — Accept mechanical `PATH_A_PASS`, but block packet authoring until Category A integrity gates clear**

I accept the mechanical PASS. Under the filed summary, `INCLUDE=8` already clears the non-empty / threshold requirement even before q013/q015 are resolved (`comp/results/griffe/prescreen/prescreen_summary.yaml:6-12`, `org/tier1_exec/3.1.0_SA_CVO/inbox/20260428T041500Z_from_1.1.0_SA_CTO_griffe_086_prescreen_pipeline_proof_validation.md:92-92`). But packet authoring should still wait. The reason is not off-ramp uncertainty; it is preregistration integrity. Stage 3 makes the INCLUDE pool a load-bearing preregistered object, not a fuzzy working estimate (`docs/architecture/COMP_LEARNS_STAGE3.md:108-109`, `docs/architecture/COMP_LEARNS_INTAKE_WAVE.md:170-171`). Until q013/q015 have canonical dispositions and the enumerable override mechanism is regularized, the packet would be built on a pool that is known to be interpretively unstable. So my ruling is:
- **Mechanical PASS:** yes
- **Packet authoring before clarifications:** no

**[P0] R-CTO-2 — Reframe griffe now as a `format-confounded target`, but do not replace exact-match ratchet**

I do not recommend option (a) defer or option (b1) global semantic-grader replacement. The correct move is a constrained version of your option (c). Griffe should be preregistered as a **format-confounded target** because `disagreement_rate=0.875` is above the level where exact-match-only result reading remains self-explanatory (`comp/results/griffe/prescreen/prescreen_summary.yaml:11-12`, `comp/results/griffe/prescreen/prescreen_summary.yaml:84-92`). But V1.3 should not be rewritten mid-target to make semantic-aware grading the ratchet; the doctrine currently makes exact-match primary and semantic-aware secondary for triage and characterization (`docs/architecture/COMP_LEARNS_STAGE3.md:348-350`, `docs/architecture/COMP_LEARNS_STAGE3.md:416-416`). My ruling:
- exact-match remains the target-local ratchet/champion metric
- every griffe primary-case report must include both exact-match and semantic-aware results side by side
- no griffe exact-match lift may be promoted to a program-level “knowledge delivery” claim unless the semantic-aware surface corroborates the direction
- if exact-match and semantic-aware outputs diverge materially at result time, interpret that target-phase result as format-elicitation-sensitive, not as clean proposition evidence

That preserves V1.2 governance continuity while making the claim boundary explicit before iter work starts.

Option A is right, with one discipline note. Do not throw the work away. Q1’s “mechanical PASS vs packet authoring” distinction is well-formed. Q2’s option (c), however, needs tightening: “no single ratchet” is too broad relative to current doctrine. V1.3 already settled the governance split in favor of exact-match ratchet plus semantic-aware characterization (`docs/architecture/COMP_LEARNS_STAGE3.md:348-350`). ## §4 — Unknown-unknowns (MANDATORY)

What you should be asking next is not only whether **post-cutoff targets** tend to show high disagreement, but whether **certain answer-surface families** do. Your own griffe summary already points that way: the disagreement is described as expected for prose-heavy enumeration/set/count outputs rather than as a general property of the repository (`comp/results/griffe/prescreen/prescreen_summary.yaml:84-92`, `comp/results/griffe/prescreen/prescreen_summary.yaml:94-102`). That means the next guardrail may not belong in target selection at all. It may belong in wave-level pool design: every future target packet may need an expected disagreement-risk profile by question family before pre-screen even runs.

Sphinx taught the program how to reject a target cheaply. Griffe teaches the harder lesson: a target can be genuinely viable on retrieval grounds and still be partially confounded on scoring grounds. That is progress, not contradiction. It means the intake pipeline is now strong enough to expose second-order validity problems instead of only first-order target-selection problems.
