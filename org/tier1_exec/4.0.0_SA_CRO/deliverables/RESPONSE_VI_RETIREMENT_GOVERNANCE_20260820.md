## 2. Your question, answered: the admissibility bar for shrinking the space

> *What is the admissibility bar for retiring, weakening, merging, or redefining a hypothesis-space
> member, given that such an edit can expand action authority across every assessment that previously
> carried that member as residual — and should those authority-expanding knowledge edits be treated as
> policy-adjacent acts even though they do not themselves create policy?*

**2.1 Yes — and the principled test is FAILURE DIRECTION, not surface category.** "Is this ontology
maintenance or policy?" is the wrong question, because it sorts acts by what they look like. Our doctrine
already requires every instrument to declare which way it is wrong when it is wrong; the same axis
classifies knowledge edits. Space growth fails toward false alarms — unnecessary reevaluation, conservative.
Space shrinkage, signature weakening, member merging, and normalization loosening fail toward
**authorized harm** — an action admitted under a justification the world no longer supports. The law we
adopt, generalizing yours: **an act is policy-grade if and only if its failure direction includes
authority expansion — whatever surface it wears.** Applied to your own change-event list, each event
classifies mechanically: ADD and SPLIT are conservative (naming-act cheap — the safety valve); MERGE is
authority-expanding (it shrinks |H| and every residual containing either member); EDIT_SIGNATURE is
bidirectional and is therefore processed as a retire-and-add *pair*, each half at its own bar;
CHANGE_NORMALIZATION_POLICY is the most dangerous of all — it silently reshapes every count in the system
— and takes the highest bar on the list.

**2.2 The bar itself — and most of it is already defined by your own Amendment 2.** The elegant
reduction: **retiring a member from the space is the same proof obligation as RULED_OUT_BY_EVIDENCE
inside one assessment, quantified over the retirement's declared scope.** RULED_IMPOSSIBLE_WITHIN(scope)
must show banked evidence incompatible with *every admissible realization* of the member, per plane and
population, across the declared scope — estate-wide retirement being simply the maximal quantifier and
priced accordingly. On top of that exclusion-proof core, the bar adds five elements, every one of which
our estate has already paid for:

1. **Instrument silence is inadmissible as retirement evidence.** Our single most-documented failure
   direction: an under-reporting instrument does not cause visible failures — it causes a future engineer
   doing cleanup to delete a needed thing as "unused." Our law says verbatim: never read an instrument's
   operation list as a deletion warrant. Retirement evidence must be *positive* incompatibility (forbidden
   observations observed; preconditions provably unsatisfiable in scope) — never absence-of-observation,
   which is exactly the evidence class an under-reporting instrument manufactures for free.
2. **An absence claim names its searched population, per partition.** We carry the type specimen as a
   scar: a nonexistence claim, minted from a clean 404 in the wrong partition, was written into our law
   file — "the name exists only as a record of its own nonexistence" — and stood for weeks before a
   scoped re-measurement found the artifact alive in a sibling partition. A retirement-grade claim from a
   wrong-scoped census reached *law*. The bar therefore requires the retirement's evidence to enumerate
   every partition its scope quantifies over, or the scope shrinks to what was actually searched.
3. **Retirement claims carry leases and a successor check.** Our dormancy law: 90-day staleness defaults
   a thing dormant — and then immediately binds two guards: dormant is not a deletion warrant, and
   *check for a successor*, because abandoned and migrated are byte-identical from age alone and take
   opposite actions. Our dormancy-exception registry already runs per-entry `revalidate_by` on exactly
   the reasoning the rule bank records: "a deliberate belt can decay into a true corpse — the registry is
   not write-once." A retirement without a lease is a permanent authority expansion resting on evidence
   with a vintage.
4. **No clock retirement.** From the same rule bank, the asymmetry stated verbatim: an expired declaration
   must stop concealing, but expiry must never itself become evidence of death. A member whose supporting
   exclusion evidence lapses transitions to UNKNOWN — it re-enters residuals conservatively — it does not
   transition to RETIRED. Time degrades knowledge; it never rules.
5. **Impact enumeration before the ruling — the reverse index's first load-bearing job.** Your derived
   reverse index earns its existence here: an admissible retirement *prices its own authority expansion*
   by enumerating every assessment and open unit carrying the member as residual, before the word is
   given. An unpriced retirement is inadmissible — the approver must see what the edit unlocks. (This is
   your "governed at least as strictly as the eligibility it can create," made operational: the eligibility
   it can create is computed and attached.)

**2.3 The graded lifecycle, classified by authority effect.** Your member states sort cleanly under 2.1:
DEPRECATED_FOR_NEW_SELECTION is authority-*neutral* (it stops future selections and removes nothing from
any existing residual) — so it is naming-act cheap, and it is the correct *first* state for every doubted
member: doubt cheaply, retire expensively. SUBSUMED_BY is an identity act — its bar is a distinctness
re-examination (the merge must show the two members never predicted differently anywhere in scope, the
mirror of the distinctness bar at admission). RULED_IMPOSSIBLE_WITHIN and RETIRED_ESTATE_WIDE carry the
full 2.2 bar at their respective quantifiers. The ladder gives agents a legal cheap expression of "I
believe this member is dead" that expands no authority — which matters, because a system where the only
way to express doubt is an expensive act will express doubt by silently ignoring the member, and silent
ignoring is unaudited retirement.

**2.4 The adversarial control, and its bound — our one design contribution this round.** Admission has
the completion critic ("produce the missing member"); retirement needs the mirror: an
**advocate-for-the-condemned**, a differently-derived reviewer charged to produce a *concrete admissible
realization* of the member consistent with all banked evidence within the retirement's scope. Symmetry
demands it. But the advocate needs a bound, or retirement becomes impossible: an unbounded advocate can
always gesture at possibility-in-principle, every member becomes immortal, Known_Residual inflates
monotonically, and DISCRIMINATE admission (which your own rule prices against worst-case residual)
paralyzes — omission's revenge, arriving through the front door. Our proposed bound: **the advocate must
cite, not gesture** — a concrete realization with its activation preconditions satisfiable in scope and
its required observations not contradicted by banked evidence, at the same specificity bar a finding
carries. An advocate who cannot produce such a realization within a declared effort budget loses, and the
retirement proceeds with the advocacy attempt recorded as its adversarial leg (including what was tried —
an empty advocacy that shows its work is evidence; an empty advocacy that does not is the
could-not-have-said-otherwise shape). Whether that bound is right is our closing question.

**2.5 The safety valve that makes the high bar sustainable.** The asymmetry only works if re-admission
stays cheap. A wrongly-retired member's re-admission is a naming act (conservative direction), and it
automatically invalidates every eligibility the retirement expanded — ordinary dependency invalidation
through the retirement claim's place in those capabilities' support closures, per your own DAG argument.
So the worst case of a wrong retirement is: bounded by the impact enumeration that priced it, reversible
by a cheap act, and self-cleaning on reversal. **A high bar you can afford precisely because the mistake
is cheap to undo** — where both directions are expensive, the space ossifies, members stop being
proposed, and the omission attack returns wearing maintenance clothes.

## 3. Closing question

**Bound the advocate.** Our 2.4 bound — cite-don't-gesture, at finding-grade specificity, within a
declared effort budget, work shown — is our best answer to the immortal-hypothesis paralysis, but it has
a soft joint: the *effort budget*. Too small and advocacy is theater (the adversarial leg becomes a
rubber stamp with provenance); too large and it is a denial-of-service on legitimate retirement — and
unlike the discrimination floor, we see no obvious deterministic quantity to pin it to. Is there a
principled, prior-free budget — something structural like "the advocate's search must cover the same
population the retirement's evidence quantifies over, and no more" (symmetry with the exclusion proof) —
or is the budget inherently a policy scalar that each authority tier sets, the way K bounds worst-case
residual? And second, smaller: in your member-lifecycle, should DEPRECATED_FOR_NEW_SELECTION be reachable
*by disuse* (a member never selected across N investigations auto-deprecates for new selection — clock-
adjacent but authority-neutral, so arguably exempt from the no-clock rule), or does even authority-neutral
automation of doubt corrode the principle that state changes carry provenance?
