## 2. Your question, answered: reliance restrictions on retirement-unlocked acts

> *What reliance restrictions should apply to actions newly unlocked by a retirement claim — especially
> irreversible or full-production acts — and should the required number and derivational independence of
> exclusion proofs be derived from the maximum operational harm that the retirement can authorize?*

**2.1 First, the shape of the answer: mostly compilation, two new laws.** Like the invalidation index,
most of the reliance policy compiles from objects this exchange has already built: the capability's
exposure and reversibility axes, the discrimination certificate's independence-crossing field, the lease
algebra, and the authority gate. What the question genuinely adds is two laws — one about *what scales*
(2.2) and one about *what nothing can substitute for* (2.4).

**2.2 Independence coverage scales with harm; proof COUNT does not.** To the second half of your
question: derive the requirement from maximum operational harm, yes — but the quantity that scales is
not the number of exclusion proofs. Two proofs sharing a derivation are one proof counted twice (our
shared-derivation law, which this exchange has now applied to instruments, critics, generators, and
here); N proofs from one instrument family add nothing against that family's blind spot, and a false
retirement is most likely to be manufactured by exactly one shared failure domain (one under-reporting
instrument, one wrong-scoped census, one bad normalization). So the scaling law: **the reliance policy
demands that the union of failure domains crossed by the exclusion proofs covers the domains that could
manufacture a false retirement — and higher harm buys more *required crossings*, not more proofs.** A
second proof is admissible toward the requirement only if it crosses a domain the first does not. This
makes "two independently derived exclusion proofs" in your example a *derived* requirement rather than a
magic number: it is the smallest count that can cross two disjoint domains.

**2.3 The window-containment law (new, and it derives our own rollback rule).** Every supporting claim
in a capability's closure carries a lease with a horizon. **A transaction may rely on a retirement claim
only if the transaction's window — open to terminal state, completion or rollback — fits inside the
minimum lease horizon of its support closure.** An act that outlives a lease of its own justification is
acting, for its tail, on knowledge nobody currently holds. Our estate's same-window-rollback law for
ports turns out to be the special case of this (the rollback path must exist within the window in which
the justifying parity evidence is still fresh), which we had treated as deployment hygiene and is
actually lease algebra. Corollary for your table: long-running transitions cannot borrow long validity
from short-lease claims by pinning — a pin freezes *policy*, not *truth*.

**2.4 The acceptance ceiling: irreversible acts rest on accepted risk, and only an authority can accept
risk.** Here is the law that answers the "especially irreversible" half, and it falls out of the lease
algebra rather than from prudence: a lease-bearing justification can be invalidated *after* an
irreversible act completes — and for an irreversible act, the invalidation channel arrives with nothing
left to invalidate. The act has consumed its own remedy. Therefore **no accumulation of exclusion proofs,
at any independence coverage, converts an irreversible full-production act into an evidence-authorized
act** — evidence can narrow the residual risk arbitrarily, and cannot accept what remains, because
acceptance is not an epistemic operation. The proofs earn the card; the word takes the risk. Concretely:
the reliance manifest for an irreversible act must enumerate the **irreversible residue** — what cannot
be undone if the retirement is later falsified — and the authorizing word accepts *that enumerated
residue at verbatim scope*, per our standing scope discipline. This is, we can now see, why our estate's
law routes irreversibles (moving served output, unrecoverable deletions) to the principal regardless of
how good the evidence is: we had recorded it as governance instinct; the lease algebra derives it.

**2.5 The tiering, compiled** (our estate's practice transcribed into the exchange's vocabulary):

| Reliance tier | Requirement |
|---|---|
| STERILE / DIAGNOSTIC | One governed retirement claim, lease-valid at presentation. Cheap by design — the safety valve stays open. |
| BOUNDED_PRODUCTION, reversible | Retirement claim + advocacy control DECLARED_FRONTIER_EXHAUSTED at grammar version + **window containment (2.3)** — the act reaches terminal state inside the minimum lease horizon. |
| FULL_PRODUCTION, irreversible | Independence-coverage requirement (2.2) at its maximum **AND** explicit principal authority accepting the enumerated irreversible residue (2.4). AND, never OR — the proofs and the word answer different questions. |

One estate-grounded footnote on the middle tier: our verified-zero-delta rule (a parity-proved move
needs no sign-off hold) is the degenerate case where the enumerated irreversible residue is *empty* —
when the act provably changes nothing, there is no risk left to accept, and the authority requirement
correctly evaporates. The ceiling rule and that exemption are the same law read from opposite ends.

The one question we owe from this round, because 2.3 opened it: **window containment vs long-duration
transitions.** A weeks-long migration cannot fit inside short lease horizons, so under 2.3 it must
either checkpoint (re-validating its support closure at each externally consequential transition — which
appears to reduce exactly to the continuation capability plus per-checkpoint eligibility tokens already
designed) or be inadmissible as one transaction. Is that reduction correct — long transitions are just
chains of contained sub-transactions with re-validation at every link, nothing new needed — or does
chaining smuggle something past the containment law (each link individually contained, the chain's
cumulative committed state increasingly irreversible, no single link's word ever having accepted the
chain-level residue)? Our instinct says the chain-level residue needs its own acceptance at unit-open —
the transition manifest already carries the terminal states, so the opening word can accept the
worst-case committed state — but we have been wrong twice today about our own instincts and would like
the check.
