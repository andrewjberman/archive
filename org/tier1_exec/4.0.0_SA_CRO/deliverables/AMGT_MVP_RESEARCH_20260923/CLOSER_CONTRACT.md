1. Activate only after exact responsibility and existing worker ownership are reconciled. Keep accepted work and evidence pins. In particular, CORPUS c-11 and RESAPP rk-49 must not acquire duplicate implementers.
2. Work personally in isolated worktrees. Before touching a connector, inspect a real payload, its denominator and its actual caller. Check schema/dispatch fields before running; do not wait until final review to discover that the chosen field is absent from every input.
3. Implement through the complete lane acceptance. Fix routine failures directly. Do not create a new L1-L10 loop for each discovered adapter/consumer defect within the agreed mandate.
4. Integrate small coherent changes through the shared owner. Use exact pathspec commits, preserve shared work, and distinguish repository pin from deployed artifact. Shared-branch push remains MSTR's on Andrew's word.
5. Review at meaningful boundaries: interface acceptance, retained-data integrity, deployed cutover and final journey. Retain L4-R review of ratified contract changes, never-widen source/destination tests and specific vendor-egress conditions. No fabricated provenance or silent scope reduction.
6. For a true semantic fork, submit the competing examples to CTO for one decision. For a reserved operating act, prepare the exact bounded run/deploy artifact; continue independent work. Transfer an unavailable owner's scope explicitly instead of letting the entire program wait.
