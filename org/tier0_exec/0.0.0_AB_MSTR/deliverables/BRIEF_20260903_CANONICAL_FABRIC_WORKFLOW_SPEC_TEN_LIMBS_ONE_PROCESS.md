## 4. The constitutional form -- what the spine contains and what it must not

The spine is read at the start of every project by every seat. It is law, like `org/tier0/CONSTITUTION.md`, and
takes the same form:

**Contains, per limb, in this order and nothing else:** the limb number and name; the ONE owner; who is NOT
engaged; the input artifact and the output artifact, each as a path grammar under the project tree; the skill or
tool that performs it; the gate or notifier that binds it and what that gate REFUSES; the handoff to the next limb;
for L10, the three routes back and the precondition. Then one table mapping limb to owner to `dep_stage` to
refuser. Then the amendment protocol. Then a changelog of one line per version.

**Must not contain:** incident narratives; quotes from Andrew or from any seat; dates inside the body (the
changelog carries dates); strikethroughs, VOID markers, "corrected on", "measured 2026-xx-xx", "this sentence was
false for six minutes"; a seat's self-corrections or apologies; message ids; commit hashes inside the body (the
changelog line cites the ruling record by hash); "I do not know which governs" or any sentence that names a
second process. Rationale is one clause where it is load-bearing, never a paragraph.

**Where the removed material goes:** rulings and their verbatim words to `org/tier0/ledger/` and the Andrew-direct
records; incidents and measurements to `docs/references/` or the seat's log; the spec cites none of them inline.

**Amendment protocol:** a versioned edit of the whole file by the CTO (its L2-L4 owner as scribe of process law),
on Andrew's word or on a routed L10 finding, with one changelog line naming the record that authorized it; no
in-place markers, no partial-clause patches. Independent review of a law edit is the CVO's Art. XIV role and is
not an L-step engagement.
